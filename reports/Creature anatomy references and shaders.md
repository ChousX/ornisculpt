# Mine free anatomy, then fix the prepass

For ornisculpt, the most useful anatomical reference is also the most legally free. **Numbers measured from any source can be committed: bone ratios, segment masses, joint ranges, wing fractions.** Public-domain atlases from before 1931 (Stubbs, Chauveau, Sisson 1910–1917, Muybridge, Wiedersheim, Gadow, Snodgrass 1925) and CC0 museum collections (the Met, Smithsonian 2D and 3D) cover horses, domestic quadrupeds, birds and reptile girdles well enough to commit the images themselves. Modern artist books, MorphoSource CT scans and the SMAL shape models are restricted to reference use. Coverage is thinnest for bats, lizard scalation and insects outside Snodgrass. On rendering, one fix outranks every new shader: **`CoatExt` does not deform geometry in Bevy's prepass, so depth, normals, motion vectors and shadows all see the undeformed rest pose.** Until it does, TAA ghosts, SSAO and contact shadows darken the wrong silhouette, and shadows come from the wrong shape. With that fixed, the biggest close-up gains in Bevy 0.20 come from ten-line shader changes inside the existing `ExtendedMaterial`: geometric specular AA, an analytic refracted eye, per-scale normal tilt with crevice occlusion, and clearcoat for wet surfaces. A photo mode that freezes time and accumulates jittered frames comes next. Bevy has no subsurface scattering, and its Solari ray tracer cannot see custom materials, so skin scattering must be approximated in the shader.

## Measurements are free to commit; images split into three licence tiers

The working rule across all sources is to separate *facts* from *expression*. Ratios measured off a photo, a scan or a paywalled paper are facts, and encoding them in `.body.ron` copies nothing protected. Tracing a figure into a committed SVG or texture creates a derivative that inherits the source's licence (this is the research team's inference, not a legal opinion). In the US, works published before 1931 are public domain as of 2026. Repo-safe material falls into three tiers. **Tier A** can be committed freely: Met CC0, Smithsonian CC0, NLM PD, Wellcome PDM, pre-1931 scans, and PhyloPic CC0/PDM silhouettes. **Tier B** can be committed with an attribution file: CC BY and CC BY-SA images such as Witton's pterosaur figures and some Hartman silhouettes. **Tier C** stays in a git-ignored `reference_local/`: anything NC, all-rights-reserved or of unknown status. PDM and CC0 need no attribution ([PhyloPic usage](https://www.phylopic.org/articles/image-usage)). CC0 waives copyright only, and other rights can still apply ([SI FAQ](https://dashboard.si.edu/openaccess/faq)).

Three cautions sit behind the table. First, the Ellenberger/Baum/Dittrich atlas is the most useful artist atlas (288 drawings of horse, dog, lion, cow, stag and goat, each in surface, muscle and skeleton views). Its only scan is a private archive.org upload of the 1956 Dover edition with **no rights field in its metadata** ([archive.org metadata](https://archive.org/metadata/an-atlas-of-animal-anatomy-for-artists-dover-anatomy-for-baum-hermann-brown-lewi/metadata)). Treat it as Tier C until an original German edition turns up. Second, Sisson & Grossman editions from 1938 onward, and the Dover Muybridge reprints, are in-copyright or lending-only scans. Take Muybridge frames from the 1887 and 1902 originals instead. Third, 3D licences change model by model even within one museum account. On Sketchfab, the NHM London whale is CC BY-NC-SA, while a Belgian institute's human skull is CC BY ([Sketchfab](https://sketchfab.com/3d-models/blue-whale-skeleton-8502dbef80ed4aa688c13c90cb14de73)). MorphoSource requires written approval for commercial use, and some of its records add "Restricted Download" terms on top of the CC licence ([MorphoSource](https://n2t.net/ark:/87602/m4/602892)). The SMAL animal shape models forbid any use in a commercial product and limit use to a single user ([AWOL licence](https://awol.is.tue.mpg.de/license.html)). They were also learned from **scans of toy figurines**, so their proportions are stylised ([SMAL](https://smal.is.tue.mpg.de/)).

Bulk access is practical. The Smithsonian publishes a weekly CC0 dump in a public S3 bucket that needs no AWS account ([AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/smithsonian-open-access)). Wikimedia Commons returns licence fields through `iiprop=extmetadata` ([CommonsMetadata](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Extension:CommonsMetadata/en)). BHL's Flickr stream holds more than 300,000 nature illustrations tagged by taxon, mostly public domain but to be checked per image ([Illinois guide](https://guides.library.illinois.edu/BHL/flickr)). It is likely the fastest route to scale, beetle and dragonfly plates.

## Biomechanics tables turn sliders into plausible bodies

The numeric sources map directly onto ornisculpt's parametric parts. Body-segment parameter (BSP) tables give segment mass as a share of body mass, centre of mass as a fraction of segment length, and radii of gyration. Buchner et al. measured 26 segments in six Dutch Warmbloods, with standard errors of **8 % for mass, 3 % of length for CoM, and 17 % for inertia** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9165402)). The CC BY German Shepherd study puts each hind limb at **about 6.8 % of body mass**, heavier than a forelimb ([PMC6195294](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6195294/)). de Leva's human tables give a male trunk at 0.4346 and a thigh at 0.1416 of body mass ([de Leva](https://ebm.ufabc.edu.br/wp-content/uploads/2013/12/Leva-1996.pdf)), and an open Jupyter notebook implements them ([BMClab](https://nbviewer.jupyter.org/github/demotu/BMC/blob/master/notebooks/BodySegmentParameters.ipynb)). A per-archetype BSP table could check `mass.rs` output for plausibility. The horse and dog tables themselves are images or paywalled, so they still need extracting from the PDFs. No BSP data was found for birds, bats or felids.

For a size slider, the allometry is clear enough to encode. Across 37 mammals from shrew to elephant, **limb bone length scales as roughly M^0.32–0.35 and diameter as M^0.36** ([Hokkanen, citing Alexander et al. 1979](https://www.miketaylor.org.uk/dino/hokkanen/Size-Hokkanen.pdf)). Biewener found that large animals limit bone stress mainly through **more upright, columnar posture** rather than much thicker bones ([Biewener lab](https://biewenerlab.oeb.harvard.edu/publications/allometry-quadrupedal-locomotion-scaling-duty-factor-bone-curvature-and-limb)). Scaling a creature up should therefore straighten its limbs as well as thicken them. Note that Hokkanen's fitted length exponent (0.32) and the original abstract's (0.35) disagree, and neither was rechecked.

Dragons and wyverns need the pterosaur numbers most. Witton & Habib (CC BY) put *Quetzalcoatlus* at a **10–11 m span and only 180–250 kg**, with a torso of about 0.65 m. The wing finger is **47 % of wing length in *Q. northropi* and 66 % in *Pteranodon***. Launch was quadrupedal, and the humerus was more than twice as strong as avian scaling predicts ([PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0013982)). The largest living flying birds weigh 16–22 kg at about 3 m span (same source). A one-tonne, 10 m dragon is not plausible. A believable one has a small torso, a huge pectoral girdle and humerus, a wing-finger fraction between 0.47 (broad wing) and 0.66 (narrow wing), and for bat-style membranes an aspect ratio near 6–8. Most bats have half-span aspect ratios of 2.75–3.75 ([PMC5206602](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5206602)).

Joint limits should be coupled across axes, not independent per axis. A turkey elbow gives about 90° of flexion, 50° of abduction and 40° of long-axis rotation, and abduction narrows as the elbow extends ([SICB](https://sicb.org/?p=6788)). That argues for limit cones that depend on flexion. German Shepherds flex less than Labradors at every joint except the carpus ([Frontiers](https://frontiersin.org/journals/veterinary-science/articles/10.3389/fvets.2019.00424/full)), so build type matters. For muscles, the Hutchinson OpenSim models (ostrich, greyhound, T. rex, velociraptor) store origin, insertion and via points, masses, inertias and maximum isometric forces as XML in body-local frames ([SimTK](https://www.simtk.org/users/jhutchinson)). That data maps almost one-to-one onto ornisculpt's lever and muscle parts. Licences vary per SimTK project, but extracted numbers are facts.

## One broken prepass undermines every hero effect

Bevy 0.20's best close-up features all read the prepass. TAA requires `DepthPrepass` and `MotionVectorPrepass` and warns that **"failure to [write correct motion vectors] will lead to ghosting"** ([taa/mod.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_anti_alias/src/taa/mod.rs)). SSAO requires depth and normal prepasses ([ssao/mod.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/ssao/mod.rs)), and contact shadows require depth ([contact_shadows.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/contact_shadows.rs)). `MaterialExtension` exposes `prepass_vertex_shader()` and `deferred_vertex_shader()` for exactly this case ([extended_material.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/extended_material.rs)). But `src/coat/material.rs` overrides only the main vertex and fragment shaders, and deliberately strips `COAT_COR` and `COAT_MUSCLE` from prepass pipelines. Crowd DQS skinning is not reproduced there either. As a result, the prepass sees the undeformed rest pose. Shadows come from the rest-pose silhouette, the main pass depth-tests against mismatched depth, and animated limbs ghost under TAA.

The fix is to write a coat prepass vertex shader that runs the same CoR/DQS/muscle deformation. It must also compute `previous_world_position` from the *previous* frame's bone matrices and muscle amplitudes, which means a double-buffered "prev bones" storage buffer. Bevy's own prepass computes motion as `(clip − previous_clip) · (0.5, −0.5)` using unjittered matrices ([prepass.wesl](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/prepass/prepass.wesl)), and the coat version should match that. A matching prepass *fragment* shader should discard fur shells with the same strand function, so SSAO does not see a solid crust. For stills only, a shortcut is enough: freeze `Time<Virtual>` so previous and current positions coincide. Even then the prepass must deform, or depth will not match. Stay on the forward renderer. Deferred would flatten the coat's custom shading into the G-buffer, drop `specular_tint`, and forbid MSAA ([opaque.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_material/src/opaque.rs); [pbr_material.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy_pbr/0.20.0/source/src/pbr_material.rs)). The only deferred-only feature is screen-space reflections ([ssr/mod.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/ssr/mod.rs)), and it does not justify the switch.

## Skin, scales, eyes and wet surfaces live inside the extension

The non-obvious constraint on every surface shader is **shader-def gating**. `StandardMaterialKey` compiles the clearcoat, diffuse-transmission and anisotropy code paths only when the *Rust-side* base material has `clearcoat > 0`, `diffuse_transmission > 0` or `anisotropy_strength > 0` ([pbr_material.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy_pbr/0.20.0/source/src/pbr_material.rs), [pbr_functions.wesl](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy_pbr/0.20.0/source/src/render/pbr_functions.wesl)). Writing `pbr.material.clearcoat` in WESL does nothing otherwise. Today `src/mob/generate.rs` sets `diffuse_transmission = 0.5` only on the Wing layer. Ears, frills and skin translucency are therefore silently ignored, and no layer can use clearcoat yet. Setting epsilon values (`clearcoat: 1e-3`, `diffuse_transmission: 1e-3`) on skin, eye and wet layers is the zero-risk first step. Anisotropy additionally needs mesh tangents and the `pbr_anisotropy_texture` cargo feature ([pbr_fragment.wesl](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy_pbr/0.20.0/source/src/render/pbr_fragment.wesl)).

**Skin and subsurface scattering.** Bevy 0.20 has no SSS. A GitHub search finds only open issue #14487, a mesh-thickness prepass ([issue](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/issues/14487)). Jimenez's separable screen-space SSS costs about 0.49 ms at 1080p ([TU Wien](https://cg.tuwien.ac.at/research/publications/2015/Jimenez_SSS_2015)). It needs a diffuse-only render target that Bevy does not produce, which is roughly one to two weeks of render-graph work. The research team does not recommend it unless human skin becomes a hero target, since reptile and dragon scales have smaller scattering radii. The cheap route follows Penner's pre-integrated skin idea, where curvature and N·L index a scattering falloff that passes through red on tight curves ([Penner slides](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/penner-preintegrated-skin-rendering-siggraph-2011-advances-in-realtime-rendering-course/13966747)). Implement it as a per-channel curvature-dependent wrap term for the key light, added after `apply_pbr_lighting` the same way the coat adds glow. Per-pixel curvature from `fwidth` bands badly ([GameDev.net](https://www.gamedev.net/forums/topic/658069-how-can-i-have-a-softer-skin-using-brdf/5165432/)). ornisculpt has the SDF, so it can bake analytic curvature (the SDF Laplacian) per vertex at meshing time. In the same pass it can bake thickness (march the SDF along −N) for ears and membranes, plus IQ-style SDF ambient occlusion. These three baked attributes feed SSS, translucency, cavity dirt and specular occlusion at near-zero runtime cost.

**Specular AA first.** Filament's geometric specular AA widens roughness by the screen-space variance of the normal, with a default variance of 0.15 and threshold of 0.2 ([Filament Materials](https://google.github.io/filament/Materials.md.html)), following Tokuyoshi & Kaplanyan ([JCGT 2021](https://www.jcgt.org/published/0010/02/02/paper-lowres.pdf)). It is about ten lines in the extension and must run in uniform control flow. Pair it with a Toksvig-style step. Where the existing `detail()` fades a bump, add the lost slope variance to roughness instead of discarding it, so distant scales read satin rather than mirror-smooth. This is the best stability gain per line of code for scales, eyes and membranes.

**Scales.** The project's `cellular()` already returns F1, F2 and a cell id. Use F2−F1 for crisp, pixel-width-aware crevice masks. Use an asymmetric dome for imbricate (overlapping) scales. Drive hue and roughness jitter from the cell hash, and add a **2–5° per-scale normal tilt**, so each scale catches light separately. That tilt is the cheapest large close-up win. Feed the crevice mask into `pbr.diffuse_occlusion` and `specular_occlusion` (both writable in `PbrInput`, [pbr_types.wesl](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy_pbr/0.20.0/source/src/render/pbr_types.wesl)) so highlights stop glowing out of cracks. For snakes, beetles and iridescent wyvern scales, replace F0 with a thin-film Fresnel in the spirit of Belcour & Barla's microfacet iridescence model ([Unity blog](https://blog.unity.com/es/technology/a-practical-extension-to-microfacet-theory-for-the-modeling-of-varying-iridescence)). Write it into `reflectance` or `specular_tint`, not albedo. A cheap three-wavelength RGB phase evaluation works first; port the analytic spectral integration later if banding shows.

**Wet surfaces and mucous membranes.** Clearcoat follows Filament's layered model: a coat with F0 0.04 attenuates the base layer by the coat Fresnel ([Filament](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)). Bevy carries a separate `clearcoat_N`. A wet film should darken albedo, lower base roughness on non-porous areas, and use a coat normal blended toward the macro normal, so the film smooths over scale bumps while the bumps stay visible underneath. Nose leather gets cellular tubercles under clearcoat 0.6–1.0. Mouth interiors need strong baked AO or they glow. The darkening constants are common heuristics, not sourced measurements.

**Eyes deliver the most perceived realism per hour.** Jimenez's GDC 2013 character talk put eye shading next to SSS as a core realism cue ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1018270/Next-Generation-Character)). The recommended analytic recipe works in eye-local space with no textures. It refracts the view ray through a spherical-cap cornea (IOR about 1.376) onto an iris plane. It builds the iris in polar coordinates with a parametric pupil: round, vertical slit, horizontal bar or W. It adds a darkened limbal ring, a caustic on the side away from the light, and lid AO from the head SDF. The cornea specular comes through `clearcoat_N`, a separate coat normal, which is the same trick a commercial UE eye pack uses with its "second normal" ([Fab](https://www.fab.com/listings/31c329e0-58a1-4e1e-ac29-0a3b3678299f?lang=es-mx)). A coaxial-light retroreflection inside the pupil adds tapetum eyeshine. Treat the iris-depth and IOR constants as unverified, since neither the slides nor the MetaHuman docs could be read.

**Membranes.** The wing shader already has veins, fast translucency and thin-film for insects. Three upgrades remain. Replace the fixed glow tint with Beer–Lambert transmittance `exp(−σ·thickness)`, with σ low in red, so thin membrane glows orange-red and veins block light. Add `TransmittedShadowReceiver` so the back lobe receives shadows. Multiply the fast-translucency term by the directional shadow, so wings stop glowing through occluding bodies. Bevy offsets the back-lobe lookup by `thickness` along −N ([pbr_functions.wesl](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy_pbr/0.20.0/source/src/render/pbr_functions.wesl)), so that value should come from the baked thickness.

## Fur and feathers force a choice between MSAA and TAA

Shells alone show slicing at silhouettes and grazing angles. The established fix is **shells plus silhouette fins**, from Lengyel et al.'s 2001 real-time fur ([Hoppe](https://hhoppe.com/proj/fur/)). wgpu has no geometry shaders, so build a static fin mesh on the CPU from the mesh edge list, skin it with the same joints, and fade fins by `1 − |N_face·V|`. Shell count should follow on-screen fur length, at roughly one shell per 1–2 px of projected length (8–64 shells). The current default is 12 shells over 2.5 cm. For close-ups, the research team recommends a hybrid matching real coats, which pair a dense undercoat with coarse guard hairs ([DTU hybrid fur](https://people.compute.dtu.dk/jerf/papers/hybrid_fur.pdf)). Keep shells for the undercoat and add **2–8k camera-facing ribbon guard hairs** rooted on the deformed skin. That is about 128k triangles per creature, and fill rate, not vertex count, sets the cost. Shade the ribbons with Kajiya-Kay plus Scheuermann's shifted, tinted second lobe ([Scheuermann 2004](https://history.siggraph.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/2004-Talks-Scheuermann_Practical-Real-Time-Hair-Rendering-and-Shading.pdf)). Root AO by shell height, `mix(0.3–0.5, 1, h)`, is the single biggest depth cue. Full strand rasterisers now reach about 127k strands in 3.1 ms on an RTX 5080 ([arXiv 2607.04230](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04230)). In Bevy, though, that is a multi-week custom pipeline, which a sculpting tool does not need. Short coats such as horses barely benefit from shells. Use `StandardMaterial` anisotropy along the comb direction instead ([PR #13450](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/pull/13450)), and write `ATTRIBUTE_TANGENT` from the comb field rather than from MikkTSpace on the UV-less surface-nets mesh. Manes and tails need cards or ribbons.

The alpha strategy hits a hard conflict. `AlphaToCoverage` gives soft, stable, sort-free strand edges, but it **requires MSAA and degrades to a 0.5 mask without it** ([AlphaMode docs](https://dev-docs.bevy.org/bevy/prelude/enum.AlphaMode.html)). TAA requires `Msaa::Off`, and Bevy's OIT **panics** when MSAA is on ([oit/mod.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy_core_pipeline/0.20.0/source/src/oit/mod.rs)). OIT is a poor fit for fur either way, since shell overdraw exceeds its default four fragments per pixel. The recommended resolution is a TAA hero mode with **hash-dithered alpha testing**. Its stochastic noise is exactly what TAA integrates, and SSAO and PCSS soft shadows want TAA too. Keep an MSAA×4 + A2C mode as the fallback for when motion vectors are untrustworthy. In photo mode the conflict disappears, because frame accumulation (below) resolves dithered alpha on its own. Outer shells should be `NotShadowCaster`, since every shell casts a binary alpha-tested shadow and many shells cause acne ([render/light.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/bevy_pbr/0.20.0/source/src/render/light.rs)). Contact shadows can supply the near-surface darkening instead.

Real-time feathers are textured cards. Their rachis, vane outline and barbs can be generated procedurally in the card's (across, along) UV, with barbs as `fract((along + |across|·tanθ)·n)` sharpened by `fwidth`. The barb tangent is known analytically, so it can drive anisotropic sheen. The 2026 TOG model by Chen et al. confirms that procedural barb and barbule encoding gives multiscale appearance with correct self-shadowing ([Melbourne record](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/2331275-a-real-time--multiscale-and-procedural-feather-appearance-model)), but it publishes no frame times. Weta's eagle used 2.5M strands for barbs in an offline-quality context ([befores & afters](https://beforesandafters.com/2020/11/18/eagle-vs-meerkat-behind-weta-digitals-stunning-unreal-engine-short/)), which shows what card-based feathers give up.

## A hero camera, a soft rig and a photo mode finish the shot

The `3d` feature already includes the anti-aliasing, post-processing and tonemapping LUTs. Hero work also needs the opt-in features `experimental_pbr_pcss`, `area_light_luts`, `bluenoise_texture`, `hdr` and `pan_orbit_camera` ([Cargo.toml](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/Cargo.toml)). Gate them behind a project `hero` feature so normal builds stay lean. The table lists the recommended hero settings, all verified against 0.20 source.

| Small-scale shadows | `ContactShadows` on camera + `contact_shadows_enabled` on key light | Scale crevices, eyelids ([contact_shadows](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_pbr/src/contact_shadows.rs)) |
| Shadow maps | `DirectionalLightShadowMap { size: 4096 }`, one tight cascade (5–10 m), `ShadowFilteringMethod::Temporal`, PCSS `soft_shadow_size` | Puts every texel on the subject; PCSS is noisy without temporal filtering ([directional_light](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_light/src/directional_light.rs)) |
| Lighting rig | Shadowed `DirectionalLight` key, 2×2 m `RectLight` fill (casts no shadows), rim `SpotLight`, low-intensity `GeneratedEnvironmentMapLight` from a studio HDRI | Soft wet and eye speculars from the area light; rim light shows fur silhouettes ([rect_light](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_light/src/rect_light.rs), [probe](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_light/src/probe.rs)) |
| Exposure and DoF | `PhysicalCameraParameters` shared by `Exposure::from_physical_camera` and `DepthOfField::from_physical_camera`, `mode: Bokeh`, f/2.8–5.6 | Physically consistent bokeh ([dof](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_post_process/src/dof/mod.rs)) |
| Tonemapping and grading | `Hdr`, `Tonemapping::AgX` (or `KhronosPbrNeutral` for colour-faithful shots), `DebandDither::Enabled`, `ColorGrading`, subtle `Bloom::NATURAL`, light `Vignette` | Dithering prevents banding in soft studio gradients ([view/mod.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_render/src/view/mod.rs)) |
| Lens | Vertical FOV around 20–30°, near plane about 0.01 | Telephoto framing avoids snout distortion and strengthens bokeh |

Drive autofocus each frame from the creature's eye anchor: `(eye_world − cam.translation)·cam.forward()`, smoothed. Use fixed `Exposure` for stills, so frames stay consistent. Two gaps remain. Bevy 0.20 has **no film grain**, so it would need a custom fullscreen pass after tonemapping ([effect_stack](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/tree/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_post_process/src/effect_stack)). And **Solari is unusable for creatures**, because it requires `MeshMaterial3d<StandardMaterial>` and a fixed vertex layout, and it builds its acceleration structure from the CPU mesh ([solari types.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bevyengine/bevy/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_solari/src/scene/types.rs)). That conflicts with `ExtendedMaterial<StandardMaterial, CoatExt>` and GPU-side deformation.

Bevy has no photo mode or multi-frame accumulation, so it must be hand-built from public pieces. Pause `Time<Virtual>`. Render a second `Camera3d` to an `Image` at 2–4× the output size. Then either let TAA converge over 16–64 static frames, or disable TAA, drive `TemporalJitter.offset` through Halton(2,3) samples ([camera.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_render/src/camera.rs)), and average the `Screenshot::image` readbacks in linear space ([screenshot.rs](https://github.com/bevyengine/bevy/blob/v0.20.0/crates/bevy_render/src/view/window/screenshot.rs)). Downsample with a Lanczos filter and save. This route also resolves dithered fur, PCSS noise and sub-pixel scale sparkle in one step, and it is the highest-quality output Bevy can produce today. One open question is whether `Screenshot` captures before or after tonemapping on HDR image targets. Test that before relying on linear averaging.

## Conclusion

The research changes the priority order more than the wishlist. The engine features ornisculpt wants are mostly built in: GTAO, contact shadows, PCSS, area lights, bokeh DoF and AgX. What blocks them is project-side plumbing: a prepass that does not deform, and base-material shader defs that are never switched on. So the cheapest path to hero shots is plumbing first: a deforming coat prepass, epsilon clearcoat and transmission on the base materials, and baked SDF curvature, thickness and AO attributes. Feature shaders come second, and of those, eyes and specular AA return the most. The SDF that generates ornisculpt's meshes is an underused rendering asset. It gives analytic curvature, thickness and occlusion that mesh-based tools must approximate, and it makes credible pre-integrated skin and Beer–Lambert membranes possible without screen-space SSS.

On anatomy, the strategic shift is to treat reference as data rather than imagery. The licence trap lies almost entirely in images and meshes. Ratios, BSP tables, ROM cones and OpenSim muscle paths are free to commit, and they make archetypes measurably plausible. A 10 m wyvern at 200–250 kg with a 0.47–0.66 wing-finger fraction is a testable spec, not an aesthetic guess. The unfilled gaps are bats, lizard scalation and insect morphometrics. The next research pass should target CC BY papers in PLoS ONE and PeerJ for those taxa, plus extraction of the Buchner and German Shepherd tables.
